# nanodregg stand-ins: what stands where a real thing should (2026-10-19)

Ember (2026-10-19, STALL-WATCH 09:58Z): "we do need to swap things in for the stand-ins and other fakes that we have."
This file lists every stand-in, fake, placeholder, declared-unrun path, Sim-only instrument, toy parameter,
fixture-by-construction and harness checkpoint that I found in nanodregg. For each one it gives the real
replacement, the owner layer, the cost, and whether THE BAR needs it.

**Scope and method.** I read nanodregg main at **87dff6e** on hbox (`/tank/dregg-build/nanodregg/main`), from a
read-only `git ls-files | tar` copy. I also read the root records in `~/dev/redregg/work/`. Nothing was built or
run. Each claim is marked **[read: pointer]** or **[inferred]**. Pointers are paths in the nanodregg tree unless
they start with `work/`, which means `~/dev/redregg/work/`.

**THE BAR, as the charter states it** [read: CHARTER.md §0]: two friends and a hosted Hermes, over ssh. 1000 records,
each write ≤ 5 s, a cold reopen ≤ 60 s, measured over sockets on a quiet box.

**Cost units.** *hours* = under a lane-day. *lane-day* = one agent-day in one worktree. *a box* = a Linux host with
systemd that we may change at system level. *a person* = a human other than the operator, at a second machine, over
ssh. *a key* = a real provider credential that spends money.

## Two corrections to the brief before the inventory

1. **The `bar` row does not run with a zero deadline.** Its lines carry `withinMs := some 5000` and `some 60000`.
   The zero deadline is the row's **plant**, `plant := .caught .zeroDeadline 0 .hang`. That plant is a legitimate
   control: it checks that the liveness check can fire [read: Protocol/Rows.lean `def bar`]. The actual stand-in is
   different. The row runs in process, under toy parameters, on an in-memory disk, and "the `BAR` lines are printed
   for the record and judged by nothing" [read: same docstring; decisions/perf.md PERF-1 Alternatives (a)].
2. **The member rows do not run over sockets.** NANO-VS-MINI §5.2 says "Rows with plants over real sockets"
   [read: work/NANO-VS-MINI-20261017.md §5.2], and that holds only for the `cargo:` rows. The 26 member rows run
   **in process**. Every frame is encoded and decoded, but no socket is opened, no process is spawned, and no
   sandbox is used. The parameters are the toy digest and toy signatures [read: Protocol/Rows/Loopback.lean header;
   Protocol/Rows/Sim.lean]. The things that do cross real sockets with real binaries are the cargo rows
   `client:real/join/stdio/supervise`, `attach:attach` and `launcher:isolation/broker/resident/grain`, plus
   `store-plants` over the real `host` [read: gates/runs.tsv].

## 1. Stand-ins for a real actor

**A1. The Hermes resident.** This is the largest stand-in. THE BAR names Hermes, and nothing anywhere is a model-driven agent.
- *The rows.* In `hermes` and in `use`, "hermes" is one more in-process Loopback actor. It holds a toy key
  (`key:NAME`), runs the same client machine as every other actor, and installs a fixed law text (`hermesLaw`)
  [read: Protocol/Rows.lean `def hermes`, `def useRow`; Loopback.lean header]. It is a member called Hermes, not a
  hosted resident: no grain, no broker, no `nd --stdio`, no model.
- *The launcher.* The resident in `cargo:launcher:resident` is `probe`. It sends one fixed ask (`claude-m`, 10
  tokens, `hello`) and logs what it receives [read: launcher/src/bin/probe.rs header; launcher/tests/resident.rs
  R1–R6].
- *The missing path.* A resident inside a grain **cannot reach the World today.** `Hosting.Plan.connectOf` gives a
  resident exactly one connect entry, its broker socket [read: Hosting/Plan.lean:80–83]. HOSTING-20 rules that the
  resident's World access goes through `nd --stdio` on a second, typed MEMBER socket (`/run/nd-member/<dirOf>/nd.sock`).
  It lists what is "Owed (HOSTING)": the MEMBER root in Plan.lean, grain-launch's third root with pairwise
  `rootsOverlap`, the re-emitted launch frame, and the resident row's second socket [read: decisions/hosting.md
  HOSTING-20]. `nd --stdio` itself landed (189c201) [read: git log; client/src/stdio.rs]. grain-launch still takes
  `ROOT BROKER LABEL …`, with no MEMBER root [read: launcher/src/bin/grain-launch.rs header]. No unit renders the
  member socket [read: grep of Ops/ and deploy/units/linux finds no `nd-member` or `--stdio`].
- *Stale comments.* `scripts/bar.sh` says "no hosted verb exists yet" [read: bar.sh header]. That is stale since
  189c201: the verb exists, and the plan root and a resident program are what is missing [inferred].
- **Real replacement.** A resident program run by grain-launch under its Lean plan. It reads and writes the World
  only through its member socket (`nd --stdio`, STDIO_VERBS) and asks the model only through its broker socket. The
  World holds its allowance and lifecycle. It is tested first against the fake upstream (E4), with the canary
  control kept. The program is plumbing plus a loop: it reads the World, asks, and writes the answer as a call. It
  decides nothing the kernel decides [inferred: from HOSTING-20 and law 2].
- **Owner:** HOSTING for the plan root, the launcher root, the resident program and the row. CLIENT reviews the
  STDIO surface. OPS renders the member-socket unit. **Cost:** 2–3 lane-days [inferred]. **BAR:** on.

**A2. The model provider.** Every upstream byte so far went to a rustls server at 127.0.0.1, holding a certificate from
the test's own CA [read: launcher/tests/common/mod.rs:82–86, 160; decisions/hosting.md:827–830]. HOSTING-15 says it
outright: "Nothing here has talked to api.anthropic.com, so whether that endpoint answers an HTTP/1.0 request with
`close_notify` and a body under `RESPONSE_MAX` is unmeasured. The first real call, an ops act, is where that is
checked" [read: decisions/hosting.md:868–871].
- **Real replacement:** keybroker, from its rendered unit, holding a real provider key and the production anchors,
  makes one real call. The `Host:`, body and response limits are then read against Lean's `Broker.anthropic`.
- **Owner:** HOSTING + OPS. **Cost:** hours, plus **a key and spend (Ember)**. This is outward-facing and spends
  money (CLAUDE.md pause item 3). **BAR:** on. F3 (SPKI pin) should land first [inferred].

**A3. The two friends.**
- *In the rows,* the friends are the founders `sponsor` and `operator`, plus `newcomer`, `poster` and the others: all
  actors in one Lean process [read: Loopback.lean header].
- *In bar.sh,* they are two `nd` workspaces on one machine, under one uid, with local unix sockets and no ssh
  [read: decisions/perf.md PERF-1; bar.sh header].
- *nd-attach,* the remote friend's bridge, has only been driven with "two pipes standing in for sshd" [read:
  attach/tests/attach.rs header]. "nd-attach under a real sshd" is declared unrun in every CLIENT handoff [read:
  work/CLIENT-LOG.md:130, 174, 193, 204].
- **Real replacement:** two members on their own machines. Each joins by `nd --join` from a signed invitation (that
  path is real and checked [read: client/tests/join.rs; decisions/ops.md OPS-15]) and reaches the box over ssh into
  nd-attach.
- **Owner:** CLIENT (attach), OPS (sshd config and accounts), PERF (measuring). **Cost:** **a person** (or two
  Ember-controlled machines) plus the B1 box. **BAR:** on.

**A4. The operator.** Most operator acts are real binaries: `host seal` (6df6a3f, planted through the exe at
c80db80), `host join`, `nd invite`, `enroll` and `grant` [read: git log; decisions/host.md HOST-43; decisions/ops.md
OPS-15]. Three are procedures, not checks:
- the founder's out-of-band `input HEX` comparison [read: ops.md OPS-15 CRITIQUE];
- the carry digest copied out of band [read: protocol.md PROTOCOL-42 addendum];
- the hosted join's chain rule, which "is the operator's shell, not a Rust reader" [read: ops.md OPS-15].

The second is honest by design, and so is the third, because the operator owns the box. The first is D4.
**Owner:** OPS. **Cost:** Ember runs RUNBOOK once on the B1 box. **BAR:** on, for setup.

## 4. Stand-ins in the kernel and protocol

**D1. The toy digest.** `Protocol.Rows.Sim` sets `D := UInt64`, hashes with FNV-1a, signs with `Kernel.Sim.toySign`,
and fixes the nonce `"rows-nonce"` [read: Protocol/Rows/Sim.lean]. Host-sim's sealers are "the Sim's toy MACs"
[read: Protocol/Rows/Writer.lean:7–8]. GATES-14 deleted the ungated `kernel-sim --toy` run, but kept the toy
parameters under lang-journey, host-sim, the rows and the codec teeth [read: decisions/gates.md GATES-14].

Known consequences, from the record:
- At 64-bit keys "the Sim's trie is shallow and adversarially weak" [read: kernel.md:544–545].
- `weighed` skips the logWeight comparison at a toy width [read: Kernel/Sim/Gen.lean:334–335].
- The carry-budget plant was vacuous at 8 bytes [read: gates.md GATES-14; kernel.md:2064].
- A harness ran "the toy World (8-byte chains) under" a deployed assumption [read: host.md:1367].

The pipeline is real. The parameters are toy, and that is intentional [read: Rows/Sim.lean header; memory
`feedback-iterate-inside-the-substrate`]. The stand-in is that **no member row has ever run at the deployed
parameters.**
- **Real replacement:** make Loopback generic in `D`, as Writer already is through `Params`. Then standing runs every
  member row a second time under `Kernel.Sim.Deployed.params`-style deployed Ctx (Digest, cSHAKE256, Ed25519),
