# What a member trusts

One writer orders a World's history. Members check its proposed history: [Receive.rederive](../Kernel/Receive/Rederive.lean) reconstructs each event against the member's World and checks the transition. Step count includes admitted turns and recorded refusals; height advances with admitted turns.

Trusted computation includes the member's executable, codecs, digests/signatures and custody. The operator sees the World: there is no content secrecy from the writer. Filesystem and process guarantees depend on platform contracts named in [Spec](../Spec/README.md).

A receipt proves a journal/refusal answer against particular roots; it does not authenticate the head itself. Local settlement uses follow's own World. A [foreign receipt](../Protocol/Foreign.lean) uses a head object the receiving World holds. Its soundness premise says the head belongs to a fold of the foreign World, not that it is the latest head.

A head records origin, steps, height, roots and chain. Members compare positions, sign attestations and post heads into ordinary objects. Sync --to detects differing chains at a reached position; --check also checks the attestation and member key. A head object's fork state retains conflicting evidence. These mechanisms expose incompatible histories when evidence is compared; they do not make multiple writers agree.

Comparison is not fork consistency ([PROTOCOL-82](../decisions/protocol.md)). A signed call names no prefix of the history its signer saw, so a writer can admit one member's call on two branches. Two members who compare heads learn that their logs agree up to the compared step, or get a named refusal and a witness. A member who never compares has only its own audited chain. Detection needs a comparison path across a split: an attestation passed outside the writer, or a post the writer delivers. The writer can withhold posts indefinitely, and silence proves nothing.

Genesis inputs can name certifiers and a threshold, committed in the World. [Head.certified](../Protocol/Head.lean) checks distinct listed signers, signatures, threshold and origin. A zero-threshold authority certifies nothing.

A thin joiner accepts an invitation under a sponsor key it trusts and downloads the pinned base. When that World names certifiers, adoption also requires a certificate of exactly its head. Without certifying authority, adoption uses the sponsor pin alone. A writer started with `host serve --certify SOCK` writes base.cert through keybroker after each seal; the deployed units do not pass that option yet. A nonzero authority without a valid certificate cannot complete thin adoption. Every log page carries the writer's newest certificate, and a member that re-executes compares it with its own fold. Under an authority that certifies, a page whose certificate is absent or does not verify ends the command `refused uncertifiedPage`: the comparison that could not be made is said.

A certificate shows that the holder of a listed key endorsed a head, and gives evidence when that key signs two heads at one step count. It does not show that the head is lawful: the key holder can sign one fabricated head per step count. Rely on a certificate for attribution, not for validity.

Each certifier's key can be replaced by one successor fixed in advance ([KERNEL-79](../decisions/kernel.md)). The genesis commits the digest of every certifier's next key. The successor seed is written by `keybroker --certify-keygen` to a file the operator keeps away from the writer's key file; keybroker never reads it. A rotation is a step of the World's own log: a `rotateCertifier` call signed by the successor key, admitted only when that key opens the committed digest (`notPreRotated` otherwise), committing the digest of the key after it. The operator runs it on a stopped writer with `host rotate-certifier` ([HOSTING-61](../decisions/hosting.md)). After it, the authority lists the successor and no longer lists the old key; the threshold and the number of certifiers do not change.

A consumer follows a rotation as it follows any step: it re-executes the call, so its World's authority moves only to the committed successor. Each certificate carries its signers' keys. A signer counts when its key is the slot's current key or opens the slot's committed next digest ([PROTOCOL-86](../decisions/protocol.md)), so a member that has not reached the rotation yet takes the successor's certificate, and a member past it refuses the retired key's (`otherKey`, then `uncertifiedPage`). A stranger holding only the genesis input follows one rotation this way, not two.

What happens to old certificates and receipts. A certificate is checked against the authority of the World it is checked in: a thin joiner checks a base's certificate under that base's own authority, so an old certificate stays valid for the head it signed; a member past a rotation does not take the retired key's signature on a head it meets afterwards, because that key may be the stolen one. A rotation keeps the World's origin and log, so a receipt for a call committed before it is checked as before, against a head the consumer holds. A stolen current key still signs heads that consumers who have not reached the rotation will take: there is no revocation message. A rotation does not make such a head valid; it gives the evidence, afterwards, that the retired key signed it. The signer's restart is covered separately: every head the key signs extends every head it signed before, across restarts, as long as the persisted last certified head survives with the seed.

The certificate is checked beside the sponsor pin. Founders must check the genesis inputs they intend to authorize. Browser visitors obtaining executable and sponsor key from the page server rely on that server for bootstrap.

The member-history theorem retains keyFaithful: an adopt continuation must name the trusted sponsor key. Re-deriving resumed plans does not remove this premise. Law-chain claims also have their stated reference restrictions and canonical-trie premises. Read [Spec's claims and floors](../Spec/README.md) before treating a component theorem as composed assurance.
