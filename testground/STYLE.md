# testground/STYLE.md — how the page's words are written

1. One fact per sentence. Short sentences. No sentence that only sets a mood.
2. The plain word first; the system's term once, in parentheses, where the visitor must learn it: "a slot filled later by choosing one proposed text (a candidate)".
3. Define every term in the window where it first appears (the window's Terms list), never only in a document elsewhere.
4. A table or a labelled value instead of a sentence wherever one fits: `exit | refused`, not "the command ended refused".
5. No decorative nouns and no chains of specifiers: not "the X of the Y on its own Z", not "the honest program's own fold".
6. No stage directions or narration: no "watch", "now", "the kernel names why", "by name", no em-dash asides.
7. Window titles are nouns: "Frames", "Refusal", "Tour". Buttons are verbs or nouns: "Run", "New World".
8. Name a refusal by the kernel's name, in parentheses after the plain statement: "refused (stale)".
9. Every number shown is measured or read from the kernel, with its unit: "ticks 94,500 / 2,097,152".
10. Target register: "alice writes slot 0. Her copy of the World checks the write before her key signs it. If the check fails, nothing is signed."
